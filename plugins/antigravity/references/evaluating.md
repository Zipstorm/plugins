# Evaluating candidates

How to choose the criteria candidates are evaluated on, and how to judge one profile
against them when the evaluation is saved. The [evidence rules](evidence.md) apply
throughout; interview notes and debriefs use the [status words](status-words.md) instead.

## Choosing the criteria

A requirement becomes an evaluation criterion only when it passes three checks:

1. **A profile can show it.** Working pattern, willingness, availability, pay, work
   authorisation and how someone works with others rarely appear on a profile: keep
   them as requirements judged when screening, never as evaluation criteria.
2. **The search does not already settle it.** A place, title or years of experience
   the search filters on is true of everyone it returns.
3. **It tells this pool apart.** A criterion everyone meets, or no one could show,
   wastes a place.

When a requirement fails the first check but has a visible sign, write the criterion
as that sign: "Partnership with analytics and ML engineers" becomes "Built data used
by ML or analytics teams (feature stores, ML pipelines, BI models)". Give each
criterion a question that says what in a profile counts as evidence for it.

## Judging a profile

For each criterion, one verdict and one line of evidence from the person's own profile:

| Verdict | When |
|---|---|
| Met | Their role history shows it: a title, or a role or project description that names it. A skill listed on its own is not enough |
| Partly | Shown below the level asked, or implied by job-related evidence: a listed skill, the team or product they worked on, adjacent tools (Kafka beside Spark suggests streaming), or one part of a two-part criterion. An inference's line starts "Inferred from" |
| Unknown | Nothing in the profile speaks to it |
| Not met | The profile contradicts it |

On a thing to avoid, met means the person avoids it. Inference never reaches met, an
employer alone does not show what a person did there, and nothing the evidence rules
forbid inferring (identity, availability, work authorisation) is inferred.

## Who passes

Where a run keeps some people and passes on others, a person passes unless a required
criterion is not met, or they have the trait an excluded criterion rules out, and no
trade-off the role states excuses it. Unknown fails no one, and preferred criteria
never fail anyone: they only rank the people who pass. You make the final call on
every person. Where every person evaluated is saved, no one passes or fails: their
verdicts order them.

## When there is no evidence

- Unknown never fails anyone: it is missing evidence, not a missing skill. It ranks a
  person below one who shows the same criterion.
- Name the open criterion in the person's summary as a question for the screen call.
- When a criterion is unknown for most people, the criterion is the problem, not the
  people: say so, and offer to judge it when screening and to judge a visible sign in
  its place. Change the criteria only with the recruiter's word.
