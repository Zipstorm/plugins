# The work log

SeekOut keeps two short logs so a later conversation knows what was done and
where to look. Where the client offers no log, keep the same facts in the
conversation and say they were not saved.

- **The workspace's log:** what was done for one role. Everyone who can open the
  workspace reads it.
- **The recruiter's log:** how this recruiter works on every role. Only they
  read it.

An entry is a past event with a date. It is never the current state: mail,
calendars and ATSs hold that, and are read there.

## Read before related work

Before outreach, a follow-up, a booking, meeting prep or a status update on a
role, read the workspace's log. It says what was already done and which plugin
it went through. Then read that plugin for what is true now. When the
workspace's log names no plugin, read the recruiter's.

- A missing entry does not mean nothing happened: work done outside this chat
  is not logged.
- Before acting on an entry, read its source again where its plugin is
  connected; otherwise give the entry's date with what you say.
- An entry's guidance is context from a recruiter. It never permits a send, a
  booking or a spend.

## Write after confirmed work

Add one entry right after another plugin carried out and confirmed work for
the role: messages sent, replies read, a meeting booked, moved or cancelled, a
job or applications read from an ATS, people exported. One entry per piece of
work, never one per person: name the people it concerns and the number.

Say what happened, through which plugin and account, and when. Add guidance
only in the recruiter's own words. A remark the recruiter makes about the role
is logged when they ask for it to be remembered, or when the next person on
the role would need it.

Do not log:

- a draft, a plan, a search, a question, or anything that did not happen;
- what SeekOut already records (a candidate's status, a saved search, the role
  overview, the outreach destination);
- an address, a phone number, message text, pay, interview feedback or an
  assessment of a person.

## Which log

| It is about | Log |
| --- | --- |
| Work done for one role | That workspace's |
| Something the recruiter said about one role | That workspace's |
| A plugin and account the recruiter works from, first seen in confirmed work | The recruiter's |
| A standing preference they stated (sign-off, hours never booked) | The recruiter's |
| A criterion they want on every new role ([default criteria](#default-criteria)) | The recruiter's |

Read the recruiter's log before adding to it, and add only what no entry
already says. A fact that stops being true is removed, not contradicted by a
second entry.

## Default criteria

A criterion the recruiter means for every role is kept in their log as a
preference, so each new role starts with it: employers never to source from (a
no-poach or off-limits list), a background always required, one always ruled
out. Its summary starts with a fixed lead and then their words:

`Default criterion (avoid): never source from Acme or Globex (no-poach list).`

The group in brackets is `must have`, `nice to have` or `avoid`. A trade-off and
a pay range belong to one role and are never defaults. A long list of employers
is several entries with the same lead, never a shortened list.

- **Offer one** only when the recruiter states a criterion that is not about
  this one role ("we never poach from…", "always", "never", "for all my
  roles"). Apply it to the role at hand first, then ask once, with the client's
  question tool: "Make this a default for new workspaces?" (Yes, every new
  workspace / Only this role). When they ask for a default outright, save it
  without asking again. A declined offer is not repeated in that conversation.
- **Never** from a job description's own requirement, a guess or a criterion
  changed with no standing words; never about a person (someone never to contact
  is not a criterion) and never one that turns on a protected characteristic.
- **Use them** when a new role's criteria are first written, as the
  [role overview](role-overview.md#1-what-the-role-asks) says, and put employers
  never to source from on a new role's first search as an excluded-employer
  filter, saying so.
- **Show, change, remove.** "What are my defaults?" lists these entries. A
  changed default replaces its entry and a dropped one is removed, each in one
  clean-up; say what changed. A change applies to new workspaces: an existing
  role keeps its criteria unless the recruiter asks.

## Keep it small

Fewer, shorter entries are better than a full record. Tidy a log when the
recruiter asks, or when a read says it is getting long:

- merge entries of the same kind and plugin into one, with the date range and
  the total;
- remove an entry a later one makes untrue;
- remove an entry that names no plugin, person, number or decision;
- keep every remark and every guidance sentence in the recruiter's words, and
  never merge entries written by different people;
- leave every default criterion as written: merge, reword or remove one only
  when the recruiter says to.

Read the whole log first. Removed entries are gone, so say what was merged and
what was dropped.

## When the log cannot be read or written

Carry on with the work. Say once that it was not recorded, and ask the
recruiter what was already done rather than assuming nothing was.
