# Meeting types: how to tell, and what the prep leads with

One line of judgement per meeting, from its subject, its people and what they wrote lately. When
two fit, the prep says which it may be and covers the likelier. Each type keeps the five parts
(Who, Why, Lately, Open, Raise); this note says what goes first within them.

## Intake or kickoff

**Tell.** The subject says intake, kickoff or a role's name; the other person is a hiring manager
(the ATS hiring team says so, or their messages about a role do); a job is opening or reopening.

**Leads with.** The role and where it stands (new, reopened, changed). Lately: what the hiring
manager said about it, with dates. Open: what is still undecided (level, location, pay band,
must-haves, the interview loop). Raise: the two or three decisions this meeting should leave
settled. When the role already has an intake brief, point to it in one line; do not repeat it.

## Candidate interview

**Tell.** Someone outside the company is in it; the subject names an interview, a screen or a
stage, or it came from a booking page; the ATS has their application.

**Leads with.** Who the candidate meets and what stage this is, for which job. Lately: logistics
and what was promised to the candidate (a call back, a document, an answer about timing). Open:
what the recruiter still owes them. Raise: what to confirm with them (next steps, timing). No
assessment of the candidate, no question about their personal circumstances, nothing from their
profile presented as how they will do.

## Candidate discussion (a debrief)

**Tell.** Colleagues only; the subject names a debrief, a sync or a decision about a named
candidate, usually soon after their interviews.

**Leads with.** Which candidate, which job, which stage they just finished. Lately: whose feedback
is in and whose is missing, as the ATS or the messages show, and the questions people raised. Open:
what the group has to decide and who decides it. Raise: the missing feedback to ask for and the
open questions. Never a verdict, a ranking or a summary of how the candidate "did".

## Offer conversation

**Tell.** The subject or the recent messages name an offer, terms, a counter or an approval; the
people are a hiring manager, a compensation owner or the candidate.

**Leads with.** Which candidate and job, and the state of the terms in the words the record uses:
requested, proposed, or approved, and by whom. Lately: what was asked for and when. Open: which
approval or answer is still out. Raise: what the recruiter needs from this meeting to move it on.
No number of your own, no salary history, no view on whether a counter will land.

## Another hiring-team meeting

**Tell.** Colleagues, about hiring but none of the above: a weekly recruiting sync, a pipeline
review, a headcount or planning meeting.

**Leads with.** What the meeting usually covers and what moved since the last one, from the
organizer's messages. Open: what is waiting on the recruiter. Raise: the items the recruiter owns.

## Not recruiting

**Tell.** Nothing in the subject, the people or the messages is about a job, a candidate or
hiring: an all-hands, a vendor call, a 1:1 with a manager.

**Leads with.** Who and why in a line each; Lately only where something was asked of the
recruiter. Keep it shortest of all, and do not reach for a job that is not there.
