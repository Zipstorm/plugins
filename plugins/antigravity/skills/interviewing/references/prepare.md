# Prepare questions that uncover evidence

Start with the role criteria, interview stage, time available and candidate
material already supplied. Draft now with assumptions marked. A request for a
30-minute screen could use 3 minutes for introductions, 18 for work examples,
6 for the candidate's questions and 3 for next steps; adapt to the actual stage.
A proposed agenda does not set a calendar event's duration.

For each important criterion, provide a core question, a follow-up probe,
observable evidence to listen for and a blank notes field. For production
ownership, for example:

- “Tell me about a production incident you owned from detection to recovery.”
- Probe: “What did you decide yourself, and what changed afterwards?”
- Listen for their responsibility, reasoning, trade-offs and observed outcome.

Use the same core questions and anchors across candidates. Add a probe about
this person's documented work when relevant; do not invent experience or fill
answers from their profile. A skill mention is a question to explore, not proof.
Keep candidate claims, observed evidence and interviewer judgment separate.

Ask job-related questions. Discuss interest, work arrangements and expectations
without assuming them. Use employer-approved eligibility wording appropriate to
the role; avoid salary history, health, family or other personal inquiries.
Offer an accommodation route without collecting medical details. The shared
[safeguards](../../../references/us-recruiting-safeguards.md) cover specialist issues.

During an interview, offer one useful prompt at a time when requested. Do not
record covertly, score tone/emotion or infer honesty from pauses or AI use.

For a work sample, use a short synthetic task, clear time expectations, allowed
resources/AI use and criterion-based anchors. Never request confidential past
work or an unpaid business deliverable. Specialist assessments need a qualified
reviewer; a draft rubric is not a validated selection procedure.
