# Book a meeting

For any meeting the recruiter asks to book, schedule or find a time for: an intake
with a hiring manager, a screen, an interview, a panel, a debrief. Done means the
meeting is on the calendar at a time the recruiter picked, with the people they
named and an invite that says why you are meeting; or, for someone outside the
company, they have the recruiter's booking link in an email the recruiter allowed;
or the recruiter cancelled and nothing was booked or sent. The hand-off says what
was booked, or what stopped it, in the recruiter's words.

## Who, how long, when

Find each person before reading any calendar. A candidate is one of a
workspace's candidates: read the workspace for who they are, where they stand
and the address it already holds; look up a missing address only as **outreach**
says. A colleague's address comes from the recruiter's directory or from what
they named. A name that matches several people is asked about, never guessed. The length is what the recruiter said, else 30
minutes; the window is the next two weeks of working days unless they named one
("next week", "Thursday afternoon", "mornings only"). A meeting is online unless
they named a room or a place. Reuse what the work already agreed: stage,
attendees, length and time zones.

## The invite

The subject names the meeting and the role ("Intake: Staff Backend Engineer"),
and for a candidate meeting the candidate, so the event can be found again from
the workspace's role and the person's name. The
note is a few lines anyone invited can read cold: why you are meeting, the three
or four things you will settle, and what to bring or think about beforehand. Use
what the work so far already holds (a brief, criteria, an interview loop); write
nothing the recruiter did not say or the work does not show. A candidate-visible
invite carries agenda and logistics only; private assessment material stays in
the [interviewer brief](calendar.md).

## The times are the question

Read available working hours for the requested participants and window. Give up to three mutually free times with time zones. Treat unreadable calendars as unknown and describe conflicts without revealing other meetings. For a request to find a time, return the options. If the recruiter picks one, clarify whether they want invitations sent unless they already explicitly requested booking for these attendees.

When booking is requested, confirm the intended attendees and selected slot, recheck free/busy, and follow the connected calendar’s own review flow. Report the event and joining details only after the calendar confirms creation. If the outcome is uncertain, check the event before trying again. Follow [calendar work](../../../references/connectors/calendar.md) and [external actions](../../../references/external-actions.md).

## People outside the company

Someone at another organization's address (a candidate, a referee, a contractor)
has a calendar nobody here can read, so they pick the time themselves on the
recruiter's booking page (Microsoft Bookings, or a Google Calendar appointment
schedule):

- **One outside person and the recruiter alone:** the recruiter's own booking
  page or meeting type of the right length.
- **Colleagues joining** (a panel, an onsite): the team's shared booking page, with
  a service for each kind of interview.

The first time there is no link: walk the recruiter through making one in their
calendar, step by step ([Outlook Calendar](../../../references/connectors/outlook-calendar.md),
[Google Calendar](../../../references/connectors/google-calendar.md)), then wait for
them to paste it. A shared page may need their admin to switch booking pages on
first; offer two lines they can send the admin. Use only a link the recruiter
pasted, exactly as pasted, and only one at their own organization's booking
service. Which link fits which kind of interview is a standing choice to
[remember](../../../references/memory.md) where the client can.

The email to the outside person says who it is from, what the meeting is and who
they will meet, the link exactly as given, and how to ask for another time. For a
candidate it carries logistics only. Never type out times for them to choose
from: the page is theirs to pick from. Send it as [mail work](../../../references/connectors/mail.md)
says, after the recruiter allows that email. When they book, the meeting lands on
the recruiter's calendar; nothing announces it, so look for it there when asked.

Only for a time the recruiter says is already agreed with the outside person
may you book it directly, after a read of those hours.

## Keep the workspace current

A booking with a workspace's candidate changes where they stand there. Once the
event is confirmed on the calendar, set the candidate's status in that
workspace: the status SeekOut offers for a booked meeting, else Interested when
they agreed to meet. A booking link that was only sent changes nothing until
they book. A cancelled meeting does not move a status back; the recruiter names
the status. Say what was set, or that nothing was recorded, and give the event
link in the conversation: SeekOut does not keep it on the candidate. Add one
[work log](../../../references/work-log.md) entry for a confirmed booking, with
the calendar it is on.

## Hand-off

Say what was booked (time, attendees, subject, event link) or what stopped it,
and the candidate's status in the workspace.
Offer the useful next step: draft the agenda or [interview questions](prepare.md),
book another meeting, find other times, or check whether the outside person has
booked. An email or chat message on its own, or an outreach sequence, is
**outreach**.
