# A strong market report

Start with the recruiter's question. Reuse supplied context; ask what they want
to understand only when unsaid, within the starter's three-question total.
A role-market question can begin with a JD, a title and place, or a few lines.
A non-role question, such as where a company's engineers go, skips role intake,
JD and rubric. Market exploration does not create a screening rubric.

For a broad role-market question, build one aligned market profile over the
as-written baseline and relevant agreed markets. Read its returned title,
employer, skill and seniority facts and follow useful leads. Look for a specific
composition difference that changes where or how to investigate, beyond the
largest total. Do not force a surprise or infer hiring demand, pay, availability
or hiring speed from profile supply.

Add supported negotiable requirement trade-offs only when they inform the
decision, each measured independently. A talent map may replace the profile
when geography better answers the question; do not repeat the same totals in
both. Inspect additional markets or company flows when useful, but retain only
the visuals that support the final answer, usually two. Use the
[concise report recipe](../../../references/report-building.md#choose-and-write).

Lead with the answer and its strongest evidence, then the visual that proves
it. End with one useful native next action, usually opening the recommended
search for evidence review; for a non-role question, offer a relevant
investigation instead. Do not begin sourcing unasked.

A narrow question gets only what it needs: a talent map, market profile,
requirement trade-offs, company flow or another appropriate module. Do not force
the broad role-market recipe onto it. Before handoff, read back the body and
module facts; requested but unmeasured scope stays unresolved, not zero or a
completed comparison. Deliver the latest report beside chat with a short
handoff, following the client's display guidance.
