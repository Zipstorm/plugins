# Refine without changing the brief

Inspect the returned query and mismatched rows before changing anything. Repair
ignored filters or unresolved entities first; a failed search is not an empty
one. Remove restrictions you added before proposing changes to stated criteria.
Keep liked people reachable and explain any trade-off that would exclude them.

## Pool target

For ordinary role searches, aim for 100–500 fitting people. This is a working
range, not a correctness threshold; named people, small employers, rare
credentials and count-only questions can legitimately yield fewer.

For an ordinary pool below 100, make at most one repair wave of up to three
independent variants: remove an invented restriction; move excess JD keyword
families into optional ranking while retaining required profile assessment;
or add same-level, same-function title synonyms. Preserve credential filters
and exclusions. Show the smallest fitting variant reaching 100, or the largest
if none does, beside the original and explain the changes. Do not repeat waves
to chase a number. If no faithful variant finds people, report that and the
specific limiting constraints.

A large matching pool is valid. Narrow only against the recruiter's stated
criteria or requested feedback; do not promote a preference to required.

## What-ifs

Changing a stated location, level, employer, credential, threshold or required
skill needs the recruiter's direction. Run each proposed option as its own SeekOut
search against the same baseline; label it and keep it in the role's workspace
without replacing the active search. Report before/after counts and what changed.

That is a what-if beside the search. When the record on screen is a report (a
plan's trade-offs or talent map), the same question revises the report instead.

Separate feedback about one person from a reusable criterion. Keep function
when changing seniority; add years only when supplied. A missing skill starts
as a preference unless made required. Preserve previous decisions and avoid
silently generalizing a rejection to everyone with similar background.

## Ready to shortlist?

After presenting a search, give the recruiter your best-effort read on whether
it is ready to shortlist from or worth refining once more. It is a subjective
judgment, not a rule or a measurement, and you may be wrong: say it as your
opinion ("I think this looks ready to shortlist", "I'd refine once more
first"), give the one or two reasons behind it, and never present it as a
verdict. When what the search returned is too thin to judge, say so rather
than guess.

Base it on what the search returned:

- Its company exclusions: every employer the recruiter excluded is excluded,
  and, at the very least, people currently at the hiring company (from the job
  description, the workspace or the conversation). A missing exclusion means
  refine first, naming it as the change.
- Its breakdown by company, title and location across the top results (ask
  SeekOut for it when you create or change the search): do the leading titles
  name the role, and are the employers and places the kinds asked for? A mix
  led by an off-role title, an unwanted kind of employer or the wrong place
  leans towards refining.
- The first page of candidates: do most clearly meet the required criteria
  from their own profiles?
- The size of the pool, against the working range above.
- Feedback the recruiter has given that the search does not yet reflect.

Then ask the recruiter once, with your own question tool, whether they agree,
offering two choices with your recommendation first: **Start shortlisting
now** and **Refine the search** (naming the one change you would make first). When you are already asking what comes next, put
your read and these two choices in that question rather than asking twice.

On **Start shortlisting now**, start straight away with **pipeline-management**
and its [screening](../../../references/screening.md). On **Refine the
search**, make the change and present the refined results. Start neither
unasked.
